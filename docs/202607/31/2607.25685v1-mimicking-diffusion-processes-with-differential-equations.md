## 摘要
与扩散过程相关的概率流常微分方程（PF-ODE）在基于分数的生成建模中被广泛用作确定性采样器，它可以再现扩散过程的边缘分布。虽然这种边缘匹配性质有时被认为是理所当然的，但其有效性取决于一个常微分方程的适定性，该方程的速度场由扩散的得分函数构造。我们在对漂移和得分的弱正则性假设下，考察扩散过程、福克-普朗克方程和相关的PF-ODE之间的精确数学关系。我们首先在扩散系数的最小假设下，建立了边缘密度流作为福克-普朗克方程解的存在唯一性。然后我们利用正则拉格朗日流的DiPerna-Lions-Ambrosio理论研究相应的拉格朗日问题。我们证明了流的存在性、唯一性和稳定性，并表明在得分的Sobolev或有界变差正则性以及概率流速度散度的单边界的条件下，该流将初始分布传输到扩散边缘分布上。我们为应用中相关的扩散模型所需的规律性确定了充分条件。我们的分析强调了欧拉描述和拉格朗日描述之间的根本区别。我们构造了一个反例，其中福克-普朗克方程具有唯一的密度流，而相关的PF-ODE在初始时刻不承认正则拉格朗日流，这表明密度演化的唯一性通常并不隐含确定性概率流表示的存在性。最后，我们推导了在学习到的得分近似下概率流轨迹的稳定性估计。

## Abstract
The probability-flow ordinary differential equation (PF-ODE) associated with a diffusion process is widely used in score-based generative modeling as a deterministic sampler that reproduces the marginal distributions of the diffusion. While this marginal-matching property is sometimes taken for granted, its validity depends on the well-posedness of an ordinary differential equation whose velocity field is constructed from the score function of the diffusion. We examine the precise mathematical relation between a diffusion process, the Fokker-Planck equation and the associated PF-ODE under weak regularity assumptions on the drift and score.   We first establish existence and uniqueness of the marginal density flow as a solution of the Fokker--Planck equation under minimal assumptions on the diffusion coefficients. We then study the corresponding Lagrangian problem using the DiPerna-Lions-Ambrosio theory of regular Lagrangian flows. We prove existence, uniqueness and stability of the flow, and show that it transports the initial distribution onto the diffusion marginals, under Sobolev or bounded-variation regularity of the score together with one-sided bounds on the divergence of the probability-flow velocity. We identify sufficient conditions for the required regularity in diffusion models relevant for applications.   Our analysis underlines a fundamental distinction between Eulerian and Lagrangian descriptions. We construct a counterexample in which the Fokker--Planck equation has a unique density flow while the associated PF-ODE fails to admit a regular Lagrangian flow from the initial time, demonstrating that uniqueness of the density evolution does not in general imply the existence of a deterministic probability-flow representation. Finally, we derive stability estimates for probability-flow trajectories under learned score approximations.
